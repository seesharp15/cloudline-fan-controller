## 2. The fan interface: what is known

AC Infinity does not document the controller port. The design rests on
these field reports (sources in §13):

| Fact | Confidence | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Control connector is a 4-pin Molex (5.08 mm, PC drive-power style) | High | Your brief, the AC Infinity manuals ("4-pin molex connector") and builder reports |
| One pin carries about **+10 V DC** from the fan | High | Your brief and builder reports ("+10VDC from the fan") |
| GND and speed-control pins, and their positions | Medium | One builder's mapping (pin 2 PWM, 3 GND, 4 +10 V, 1 "not used" in his build) |
| Speed line: **low = stop, released/high = full speed** (inverted when driven by a low-side switch) | Medium–high | Several ESPHome builds use `inverted: true` with a MOSFET pulling the line to GND |
| PWM frequency is not critical | Medium | 1 kHz and 5 kHz both reported working; stock-controller frequency unknown |
| Driving the line from a 3.3 V push-pull GPIO gives only about half speed | Medium | Reported "full speed is about half". This fits an input that responds to the line's average voltage up to ~10 V. |
| A tach (FG) signal exists on the 4th pin | **Unconfirmed** | No published measurement. The design accepts open-collector or push-pull, 3.3–12 V, and copes if it is absent. |

Design choices that follow from this:

- **Open-drain drive (Q1).** The line swings the full range between 0 V and
  the fan's own pull-up level, whatever that level is. That fixes the
  half-speed problem. The fan never sees more than its own voltage, and the
  Nano never sees the fan's voltage.
- **Boot-safe default.** Q1 is ON (fan stopped) unless the firmware
  actively drives D9 low.
- **A tolerant tach input.** The fan side is only ever connected to
  resistors and a transistor base. D2 only ever sees 0–5 V.
- **A procedure to confirm each assumption** before connecting:
  [VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md).

## 3. Power supply and conditioning

```
J1.4 (+10V_FAN) ─ F1 PTC 0.2A ─ D1 1N5817 ─┬─ D2 P6KE13A ─┬─ C1 100µF ─┬─ C2 100nF ─┬─► Nano VIN
                                           (all to GND)                              (+10V_P)
```

| Part | Choice | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **F1** | PTC, 0.2 A hold / 0.4 A trip, 60 V (Bourns MF-R020) | A short on the controller must not overload the fan's internal supply. The normal load of 45–60 mA is far below the hold current, even derated to about 0.16 A at 40 °C. It resets by itself. |
| **D1** | 1N5817 Schottky | Reverse-polarity protection if J1 is wired or measured wrong. It drops only about 0.3 V at 50 mA, against about 0.7 V for a 1N4001. |
| **D2** | P6KE13A TVS (V<sub>RWM</sub> 11.1 V, V<sub>BR</sub> 12.4–13.7 V) | Clamps spikes, and conducts hard on a sustained over-voltage so F1 trips. The breakdown stays below the AMS1117's 15 V absolute maximum (many clone Nanos use the AMS1117; genuine boards use an LM1117 rated 20 V). Leakage is ≤5 µA at 11.1 V. If you measure more than 10.8 V at the fan, use a P6KE15A instead. |
| **C1** | 100 µF / 25 V electrolytic | Reservoir for the OLED's current pulses and the long cable. |
| **C2** | 100 nF ceramic at VIN | High-frequency decoupling the electrolytic cannot provide. |

**Regulator check (on the Nano):**

- VIN ≈ 10 − 0.3 (D1) − 0.1 (F1 at about 2 Ω) ≈ **9.6 V**.
- The AMS1117/LM1117 needs V<sub>in</sub> ≥ 5 V + 1.2 V dropout = 6.2 V, so
  there is 3.4 V of headroom.
- Dissipation is (9.6 − 5) V × 0.05 A ≈ **0.23 W**. On a SOT-223 with the
  Nano's small copper (about 90 °C/W) that is roughly a 21 °C rise: fine,
  even in a 40 °C grow tent.
- A buck converter would save about 0.2 W, but it adds switching noise next
  to a tach input. It is not worth it at this current.

**USB at the same time:** safe. The Nano's USB 5 V and regulator 5 V are
diode-OR'd on the board, and all grounds are common. The fan's supply is
isolated from mains inside the fan, as it must be for a user-accessible
connector.

## 4. OLED supply and I²C levels

- The module is listed as a **3.3 V** part, and the SH1106 itself is rated
  for at most about 3.5 V. Many 1.3″ boards add an on-board LDO, but that
  cannot be assumed. **U2 (MCP1700-3302E)** supplies 3.3 V from the Nano's
  5 V.
  - Headroom: dropout is about 180 mV and the maximum input is 6 V.
  - Dissipation: (5 − 3.3) × 0.03 ≈ 50 mW.
  - Do not use the Nano's `3V3` pin: on CH340 clones it is the USB chip's
    internal regulator.
- **I²C levels:**
  - R1/R2 pull SDA/SCL up to **3.3 V**.
  - The ATmega328P at 5 V reads ≥ 0.6 × V<sub>CC</sub> = 3.0 V as HIGH, so
    3.3 V is a valid high with about 0.25 V of margin. This is the standard
    way to run 3.3 V I²C parts from a 5 V AVR without a level shifter. Both
    sides only ever pull low; I²C never drives high.
  - `Wire.begin()` turns on the ATmega's internal pull-ups to **5 V**. The
    firmware switches them off straight after `oled.begin()` with
    `digitalWrite(SDA/SCL, LOW)`, so the SH1106's pins never sit above its
    supply.
  - If your module already has its own pull-ups, R1/R2 in parallel give
    about 2.35 kΩ, which is still well within I²C limits at 400 kHz.
- **Encoder and keys:** these are plain switches to GND and do not connect
  to the SH1106, so the firmware uses the ATmega's internal 5 V pull-ups.
  They work whether or not the module has pull-ups of its own.

## 5. Speed output stage (Q1)

```
 fan internal pull-up ── J1.2 ──┬── Q1 drain (2N7000)       Q1 source ── GND
                                └── [R5 4.7k DNP] ── +10V_P
 Nano D9 ── R3 220Ω ── Q1 gate           R4 10k: D9/gate ── +5V
```

- **Q1 2N7000.** V<sub>DS</sub> is rated 60 V. V<sub>GS(th)</sub> is
  0.8–3 V, and R<sub>DS(on)</sub> ≤ 5.3 Ω at V<sub>GS</sub> = 4.5 V. At the
  few-mA pull-up current it drops under 30 mV, which is a solid "0 %".
  - An NPN with a 1 kΩ base resistor would work equally well. The MOSFET
    was chosen because it draws no steady gate current, so R4 alone can
    hold it on.
- **R3 220 Ω** limits the peak current into the gate capacitance
  (5 V / (220 + ~25 Ω pin) ≈ 20 mA, below the AVR's 40 mA absolute maximum)
  and damps ringing on the gate wiring. The edges stay at about 15 ns,
  nothing at 1 kHz.
- **R4 10 kΩ to +5V** is the hardware "OFF at power-up" guarantee:
  - Every AVR pin is a high-impedance input from reset until `setup()`
    runs, about 0.1–1.5 s depending on the bootloader. R4 holds the gate
    high, so Q1 conducts and the fan line is held low.
  - The firmware then writes D9 HIGH *before* `pinMode(OUTPUT)`, so the
    hand-over has no glitch.
  - Cost: 0.5 mA through R4 while D9 is low, i.e. while the fan runs.
- **Fail-safe direction:** if the Nano's 5 V rail dies while the fan has
  power, Q1 turns off and the fan runs at **full speed**. For an exhaust fan,
  "ventilate if the controller dies" is the safer failure. If you need the
  opposite, see §12.
- **R5 (DNP)** is a pull-up for fans whose input floats (VERIFICATION A2).
- **Timer1 PWM:**
  - Mode 14 (fast PWM, TOP = ICR1), no prescaler.
  - At 1 kHz, TOP = 15999, which is 16 000 steps. The firmware works in
    0.1 % (per-mille) units.
  - 0 % and 100 % disconnect OC1A and drive the pin as a constant. That
    avoids the one-tick spike that fast PWM produces at OCR1A = 0.
- **Inversion:** fan-line duty = time Q1 is off = time D9 is LOW, so the
  gate duty is `1000 − fanDuty`. This is handled in `gateDriveFor()`, and
  `FAN_PWM_INVERTED 0` flips it (move R4 to GND as well; see VERIFICATION
  A4).
- **Frequency** is set by `FAN_PWM_FREQ_HZ` (default 1 kHz). Rise time
  depends on the fan's pull-up and the cable capacitance. For example, 10 kΩ
  with 1 nF gives τ = 10 µs: 1 % distortion at 1 kHz, 5 % at 5 kHz. Keep it
  at a few kHz or less.
- **Duty mapping:**
  - Setting *s* = 1–100 % maps linearly to
    `FAN_DUTY_MIN_PCT + (MAX − MIN) × s / 100`.
  - 0 % and OFF are always 0 % duty.
  - With the default MIN = 0 the setting *is* the duty. Raise MIN if the low
    end of the knob does not turn the fan.

## 6. Tach input stage (Q2)

```
            +10V_P                                     +5V
              │                                          │
             R6 10k                                     R9 10k
              │                                          │
 J1.1 TACH ───┴── R7 10k ──┬── Q2 base (2N3904)  Q2 C ──┴──┬── Nano D2 (INT0)
                           R8 10k                           C5 1nF
                           │      Q2 E ── GND               │
                          GND                              GND
```

| Tach state | What happens |
|---|---|
| Open-collector **released** (or push-pull high) | R6 pulls the line up. Base current ≈ (9.6 − 0.7) / 20 k − 0.7 / 10 k ≈ 0.38 mA. The collector needs only 0.5 mA (5 V / 10 kΩ), so Q2 saturates hard (forced β ≈ 1.3). **D2 = LOW.** |
| Open-collector **sinking** (≤ 0.4 V) | Q2's base ≤ 0.2 V, so Q2 is off. **D2 = HIGH** via R9. The tach transistor sinks 0.96 mA (R6), well within any FG output's rating. |
| Push-pull 3.3 V high | Base current ≈ (3.3 − 0.7) / 10 k − 0.07 mA ≈ 0.19 mA: still saturated. |
| Weak low of 0.8 V | Base = 0.8 × 10 / 20 = 0.4 V, so Q2 stays off. This is why R8 is 10 kΩ and not 47 kΩ. |
| Push-pull 12 V | Base current ≈ 1.1 mA, harmless. The fan side never connects to anything but resistors and a base. |
| No tach / not connected | R6 holds the line high, so D2 stays LOW and no edges are counted. The firmware reports "NO TACH / STALLED". |

**Filtering and debounce:**

- C5 with R9 gives τ = 10 µs, which removes nanosecond spikes coupled from
  the mains-powered fan.
- In firmware, edges closer than `TACH_MIN_PERIOD_US` (1 ms, i.e. 30 000 RPM
  at 2 pulses/rev) are rejected.
- The stage inverts. That does not matter: the firmware counts falling edges
  on D2, and the period is the same on either edge.

**Speed measurement (`TachEstimator`):**

- The ISR only increments a counter and stores `micros()` of the last edge.
- Every 500 ms the main loop computes
  `RPM = Δpulses / (t_last_edge − t_previous_last_edge) × 60 / PPR`.
  That is period-based, so it stays exact at low speed (for example 45 RPM
  gives fewer than one pulse per window) and does not jitter by ±1 pulse.
- No edges for 1.5 s means 0 RPM.
- Display smoothing is an integer EMA (÷4) that snaps to the new value when
  the fan starts or stops.
- **ACT %** = RPM / calibrated maximum, clamped to 0–100.
- **PPR** (pulses per revolution) only scales the RPM figure. EC fans differ
  (1, 2, 3, 4 …), and the default is 2. Calibrate it as in VERIFICATION C5.

**Screen details:**

- The title bar is inverted while ON, so the state is readable from across
  a room.
- Bars have tick marks at 25/50/75 %, drawn in XOR so they show on both the
  filled and empty parts.
- Refresh rate is 10 Hz, plus an immediate redraw (rate-limited to 30 ms)
  after any input.

**Calibration** accepts a result only if the average is at least 200 RPM and
every sample is within ±10 % of the average. Otherwise it shows
**CAL FAILED** and keeps the old value.

## 8. Firmware architecture

```
firmware/cloudline_controller/
├── cloudline_controller.ino   hardware glue: pins, Timer1, ISRs, EEPROM, mode state machine
├── config.h                   every tunable value and pin assignment
├── fan_logic.h                pure logic: decoder, button, duty mapping, Timer1 maths,
│                              tach estimator, smoothing, EEPROM record. No Arduino calls.
└── display_ui.h               screen layouts, templated over the display type
```

**Interrupts:**

- `INT0` (tach): counter plus timestamp, about 5 µs.
- `PCINT2` (D3/D4): runs the quadrature state machine and accumulates
  detents.

Everything else runs in `loop()`, which never blocks except during the
display transfer: about 25 ms per frame at 400 kHz. Encoder and tach edges
are caught by interrupts during that time.

**Mode state machine:** `Normal` ↔ `Diagnostics`, `Normal` → `Calibrating`
→ `CalResult` → `Normal`. While calibrating, the output is forced to
`FAN_DUTY_MAX_PCT`, and the user's setting is untouched.

**EEPROM:** a 6-byte record at address 0: magic `0xC1A6`, max RPM, and an
inverted check word. It is written only when a calibration succeeds, and
`EEPROM.put` rewrites only the bytes that changed, so wear is negligible.
The speed setting is deliberately **not** stored (see §1).

**Timing:** every comparison is `now − timestamp`, which survives the
`millis()` wrap after 49.7 days and the `micros()` wrap after 71.6 minutes.
The test suite crosses both.

**Resources (estimate):**

- Flash: about 18–22 KB of 30 KB (U8g2 core plus three fonts, about
  12–14 KB).
- RAM: about 1.45 KB of 2 KB, of which the U8g2 full frame buffer is
  1024 B.
- If the IDE warns about low memory after you add features, set
  `OLED_PAGE_BUFFER 1`, which uses a 256 B buffer and is slightly slower to
  draw.

**No watchdog:** the classic Nano bootloader (ATmegaBOOT) does not clear the
watchdog after a watchdog reset, so the board resets in a loop. That
bootloader is common on clones, which is why the watchdog is left off. With
Optiboot ("ATmega328P", not "Old Bootloader") you can safely add
`wdt_enable(WDTO_2S)` in `setup()` and `wdt_reset()` in `loop()`. After a
watchdog reset the fan comes back **OFF**, which matches the power-up rule.

## 9. Testing

**Run the tests:** `make -C tests`, with any C++11 compiler on macOS or
Linux.

| Suite | What it covers |
|---|---|
| `test_fan_logic` (238 checks, built as C++11 *and* C++17) | Quadrature decoding (direction, bounce, aborted turns, invalid jumps, resync); button debounce, short/long/suppressed presses; detent acceleration and clamping; setting → duty mapping (monotonic); gate drive incl. edge cases; Timer1 TOP; tach estimator at 1.5–50 Hz, odd frequencies, timeout, restart, PPR, `micros()` rollover; smoothing; % conversion; EEPROM record validation |
| `test_firmware_sim` (5 scenarios, ~1 200 checks) | The **unmodified `.ino`** compiled against stubs of Arduino/AVR registers/EEPROM/U8g2, driven millisecond by millisecond with simulated encoder edges, key presses and a tach pulse train. It checks Timer1 registers and D9 as the fan's speed line would see them, the displayed text, and **the layout of every frame** (text inside 128×64 and no overlapping text). Scenarios: `boot1` (full user journey incl. calibration and cancel); `boot2` (power cycle: fan OFF, setting back to the default, calibration kept); `diagboot` (knob held at power-up); `wrap` (`millis()` rollover); `calfail` (calibration without tach) |

`make -C tests shots` regenerates the screenshots in `docs/img/`.

The simulation found a real bug during development. The "no tach" check
compared two `millis()` timestamps directly, which gives a false warning
after the 49.7-day wrap. It now compares elapsed times (see `tachFault()`).

**Not yet done:**

- The sketch has **not been compiled with avr-gcc or run on a Nano**. The
  environment it was written in could not download the Arduino toolchain.
  The host build covers the language level (the logic is also built as
  C++11, as avr-gcc uses), but not AVR-specific details.
- The real U8g2 behaviour was not exercised (the fake mimics its API and
  metrics).
- None of the fan's electrical behaviour has been checked, which is what
  [VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md) is for.

Expect the first Arduino IDE compile to be clean. If it is not, the error
will be in hardware glue (`.ino`), not in the logic.

## 10. Tuning reference

All values are in `config.h`:

| Setting | Default | Change it when |
|---|---|---|
| `FAN_PWM_INVERTED` | 1 | VERIFICATION A4 shows the speed line runs opposite (also move R4 to GND) |
| `FAN_PWM_FREQ_HZ` | 1000 | You measured the stock controller's frequency, or the fan hums or hunts |
| `FAN_DUTY_MIN_PCT` / `MAX` | 0 / 100 | The bottom of the knob range does not turn the fan (VERIFICATION C6) |

| `CAL_*` | 3 s hold, 12 s spin-up, 6 s sample | A very slow-starting fan |

| `OLED_SSD1306` / `OLED_PAGE_BUFFER` | 0 / 0 | See §8 and VERIFICATION B4 |

## 11. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing at all | No 10 V, D1 reversed, or F1 tripped | Measure +10V_P. Let F1 cool and look for a short. |
| Display blank, D13 toggles with the knob | I²C problem | SDA/SCL swapped, R1/R2 missing, address 0x3D, or VCC not 3.3 V |
| Display shifted 2 px with garbage at the edge | SSD1306 board | `OLED_SSD1306 1` |
| Knob changes the value backwards | Encoder orientation | `ENC_REVERSE 1` |
| Value jumps 2 per click, or every other click is ignored | An encoder that does not rest at A=B=1 (unusual for EC11 20/20) | Check the module's encoder type. The decoder expects full-cycle detents. |
| Fan runs at full speed while the screen says OFF | Q1 not switching: gate not driven or pinout wrong | Check the 2N7000 orientation (S-G-D). D9 should be 5 V while OFF. |
| Fan runs at full speed briefly at power-up | Polarity inverted, or R4 missing | VERIFICATION A4 and B6 |
| Fan speed is backwards (higher % = slower) | Polarity | `FAN_PWM_INVERTED 0` and R4 to GND |
| Speed tops out at about half | Speed line not reaching the fan's high level | Is Q1 really off at 100 %? Is something else loading the line? Should R5 be fitted? |
| "NO TACH / STALLED" while the fan clearly turns | No tach on pin 1, wrong pin, or the tach level never goes low enough | Diagnostics: does **n=** count? Recheck VERIFICATION A5. Check Q2 orientation (E-B-C). |
| "NO TACH" only at low settings | The fan genuinely stalls below a threshold | Raise `FAN_DUTY_MIN_PCT` |
| ACT reads more than SET everywhere | Not calibrated (default 2000 RPM too low) | Calibrate (hold the knob 3 s while ON) |
| RPM is an exact multiple of the real speed | Wrong PPR | `TACH_PULSES_PER_REV` |
| CAL FAILED | No tach, or a speed that swings more than ±10 % (gusty ducting) | Check the tach. Calibrate with steady airflow. |
| Random resets when the fan starts | Supply dips | Add a bigger C1 (220–470 µF). Check the cable. |

## 12. Limitations and possible extensions

- **Open-loop speed.** The knob sets the duty, not an RPM. A PI loop that
  holds a target RPM (using the ACT figure) would be a small addition in
  `loop()`.
- **Power-loss behaviour.** If the controller loses its 5 V rail while the
  fan has power, the fan runs at full speed (§5). For fail-off instead:
  - Swap Q1 for a P-channel high-side switch, or add a normally-closed path.
  - The cheapest option is a second 2N7000 driven from +10V_P through a
    divider, which holds the line low whenever the Nano's 5 V is missing.
    That needs its own verification.
- **No mains switching.** "OFF" means speed command 0. The fan's own
  electronics stay powered, as with the stock controller.
- **Setting not stored**, by requirement. To store it, write `gSetting` to
  EEPROM a few seconds after the last change, to limit wear.
- **Possible extras:** the spare pins (D8, D10–D12, A0–A3) could carry an
  SHT3x temperature/humidity sensor for automatic control, a serial
  protocol for Home Assistant, or a second fan channel.
